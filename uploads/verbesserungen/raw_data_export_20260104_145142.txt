# 1. Relevant Legal & Regulatory Norms

- **ISO 10002:2018 (Customer Satisfaction – Guidelines for Complaints Handling in Organizations)**
  - Requires a documented, accessible, fair, and timely complaints process with clear role separation, root cause analysis, and continual improvement.
- **Segregation of Duties (SoD) Principles**
  - Internal control best practice and often required by German/EU corporate governance (e.g., KonTraG, HGB, IDW PS 330).
- **Role-Based Access Control (RBAC)**
  - Implied by internal policies; ensures only authorized personnel perform restricted actions.
- **German Civil Code (BGB) – Warranty Rights**
  - Requires timely, fair, and transparent handling of warranty claims.
- **EU GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)**
  - Requires proper handling of customer data in all process steps (implicit for any customer-facing process).

# 2. Compliance Assessment

- The process includes multiple variants for handling warranty requests, with steps for registration, examination (casual/thorough), ticket checking, decision, reinitiation, compensation, and rejection.
- Explicit restrictions:
  - The person who handles the request cannot decide its outcome.
  - Only "Sara" is authorized to make decisions.
  - Only "Sean" is authorized to conduct thorough investigations.
  - Every ticket must be checked before any investigation.
- ISO 10002 requires:
  - Clear documentation and transparency.
  - Defined roles and responsibilities.
  - Timely resolution and learning from complaints.
- Process KPIs reveal multiple variants and possible loops, which may increase risk of non-compliance with role segregation and process transparency.

# 3. Identified Violations & Gaps

1. **Segregation of Duties Violation (SoD)**
   - No explicit evidence that the person registering or examining a request is prevented from deciding the outcome, except by policy. Process data shows "decide" is performed after "examine casually/thoroughly" but does not guarantee resource separation.
2. **Role-Based Restriction Violation**
   - Only "Sara" may decide and only "Sean" may examine thoroughly, but process data shows multiple resources and no explicit mapping of activities to resources. Risk of unauthorized personnel performing restricted actions.
3. **Activity Sequence Violation**
   - "The ticket must always be checked before investigation." In some variants, "examine casually" or "examine thoroughly" may occur before "check ticket" (e.g., "register request, examine casually, check ticket..."), violating the mandated sequence.
4. **Documentation and Transparency Gap (ISO 10002)**
   - High process variant diversity and looping (e.g., reinitiate requests) may lead to inconsistent documentation, lack of transparency, and difficulty demonstrating compliance with ISO 10002's requirements for clear, documented, and auditable complaint handling.

# 4. Compliance Improvement Recommendations

1. **Enforce Segregation of Duties via Workflow Controls**
   - Implement digital workflow controls that prevent the same user from both examining and deciding on a request. Use system-enforced SoD checks at runtime.
   - Example: If "Pete" registers/examines a request, the system must block "Pete" from accessing the "decide" action for that case.

2. **Strict Role-Based Task Assignment**
   - Hard-code activity-resource mapping:
     - Only "Sara" can perform the "decide" activity.
     - Only "Sean" can perform "examine thoroughly."
   - Implement system checks to prevent task assignment to unauthorized users.

3. **Activity Sequence Enforcement**
   - Modify process logic so that "check ticket" is always completed before any "examine" activity (casual or thorough).
   - Use workflow automation to block initiation of any investigation before ticket checking is confirmed in the system.

4. **Process Variant Reduction and Documentation Enhancement**
   - Analyze process variants and eliminate unnecessary loops and reinitiations unless explicitly justified (e.g., additional evidence from customer).
   - Standardize process documentation templates for all cases, ensuring each complaint is logged, tracked, and auditable per ISO 10002.
   - Implement real-time dashboards to monitor compliance with sequence, role, and SoD constraints.

5. **ISO 10002 Alignment**
   - Regularly audit the process for compliance with ISO 10002 principles: accessibility, objectivity, timeliness, confidentiality, and continual improvement.
   - Train staff on ISO 10002 requirements and internal policies regarding role and sequence restrictions.

6. **Data Protection (GDPR)**
   - Ensure all customer data in the process is handled per GDPR, with access logging and data minimization.

## 1. Identified Cost Drivers

- **A. High Resource Usage Due to Process Variants and Loops**
  - Multiple process variants and frequent reinitiation steps (loops) increase total process events and resource touchpoints per case.
- **B. Manual and Redundant Activities**
  - Manual handling of routine steps (e.g., ticket checks, data collection) and repeated reviews (e.g., “examine casually” followed by “examine thoroughly”).
- **C. Inefficient Task Allocation**
  - Specialized resources (Sara for decisions, Sean for thorough examinations) are not optimally utilized, leading to idle time and bottlenecks, while other staff perform less specialized, repetitive tasks.

## 2. Root Cause Analysis

- **A. High Resource Usage Due to Process Variants and Loops**
  - The process allows cases to loop back (e.g., “reinitiate request”), causing the same or similar activities to be performed multiple times per case, multiplying labor and overhead costs.
  - Six unique process variants with only six cases indicate high variability and lack of standardization.
- **B. Manual and Redundant Activities**
  - Activities like “check ticket” and “examine casually” are performed repeatedly, sometimes without adding value, especially if a thorough examination is ultimately required.
  - Lack of automation in data collection and ticket checking increases manual workload and error risk.
- **C. Inefficient Task Allocation**
  - Strict role assignments (e.g., only Sara can decide, only Sean can thoroughly examine) are necessary for compliance, but non-specialized tasks are not systematically assigned based on efficiency or workload balancing.
  - No digital support for workflow routing or pre-screening, leading to suboptimal resource utilization.

## 3. Redesigned Process Flow

### **Redesign Patterns Applied:**
- Apply Integral Technology
- Apply Triage
- Eliminate Activities
- Establish a Case-Based Mindset

### **Concrete, Cost-Reducing Changes:**

1. **Digital Front-End for Single-Touch Data Collection (Apply Integral Technology, Establish a Case-Based Mindset)**
   - Implement a digital warranty request portal that collects all required information and documentation at the first customer contact.
   - Automated validation ensures completeness, reducing the need for follow-up and reinitiation.

2. **Automated Ticket Pre-Check (Apply Integral Technology, Eliminate Activities)**
   - Use workflow automation to verify ticket validity and eligibility before any human review.
   - Only pass valid, complete cases to human resources, eliminating redundant manual “check ticket” steps.

3. **Triage-Based Routing for Examination (Apply Triage)**
   - Implement automated triage logic:
     - If the case meets clear, low-risk criteria, route directly to “examine casually” by any available resource.
     - If the case is complex or high-value, route directly to Sean for “examine thoroughly.”
   - Remove the possibility of escalation from “casual” to “thorough” within the same case—triage ensures the right path from the start.

4. **Digital Workflow Routing with Role-Based Controls (Apply Integral Technology)**
   - Embed compliance restrictions in the workflow: only Sara can “decide,” only Sean can “examine thoroughly.”
   - Workflow system automatically assigns tasks to the correct resource, eliminating manual handoffs and idle time.

5. **One-Touch Resolution and Elimination of Loops (Eliminate Activities, Establish a Case-Based Mindset)**
   - Cases are resolved in a single pass: after examination and decision, the process ends with either “pay compensation” or “reject request.”
   - “Reinitiate request” is eliminated—if information is missing, the digital portal requests it up front or rejects incomplete submissions.

6. **Resource Optimization and Task Pooling**
   - Pool non-specialized tasks (e.g., “examine casually”) among all available resources except those with exclusive roles (Sara, Sean).
   - Use digital dashboards to balance workload and minimize idle time.

### **Optimized Process Flow:**

1. **Customer submits warranty request via digital portal (all info collected up front)**
2. **Automated ticket pre-check**
   - If invalid/incomplete: auto-reject, notify customer.
   - If valid: proceed.
3. **Automated triage**
   - Simple/standard case: assign to available resource for “examine casually.”
   - Complex/high-value case: assign to Sean for “examine thoroughly.”
4. **Examination**
   - “Examine casually” (any resource except Sara/Sean)
   - OR “Examine thoroughly” (Sean only)
5. **Decision (Sara only)**
   - System routes case to Sara for decision.
6. **Outcome**
   - “Pay compensation” or “Reject request” (system auto-notifies customer, closes case)

**No loops, no reinitiation, no redundant manual steps.**

## 4. Expected Cost Savings

- **Reduction in Process Events:** By eliminating loops and redundant manual steps, expect a reduction in total process events per case by at least 30–40%.
- **Resource Savings:** Pooling non-specialized tasks and automating pre-checks reduces manual workload for all resources except those with exclusive roles, freeing up at least 1–2 FTEs for higher-value activities.
- **Process Standardization:** Reducing from 6 variants to 2–3 standardized flows will cut coordination and training costs by ~50%.
- **Digital Automation:** Initial investment in digital tools will be offset by ongoing labor savings, estimated at 20–30% reduction in process cost per case, based on industry benchmarks for workflow automation in warranty handling.
- **Faster Case Closure:** Eliminating rework and manual follow-ups reduces average handling time per case, further lowering labor costs.

# 1. Identified Performance Issues

- **A. Excessive Throughput Time:** The process is perceived as too slow, with customers complaining about long wait times.
- **B. High Process Variability and Rework:** Multiple process variants exist, with some cases looping through reinitiation, repeated examinations, and ticket checks.
- **C. Inefficient Resource Utilization:** Specialized resources (e.g., Sara for decisions, Sean for thorough investigations) are not optimally leveraged, leading to bottlenecks and unnecessary waiting.

# 2. Root Cause Analysis

- **A. Excessive Throughput Time:**
  - Manual handling of routine steps (e.g., ticket checks, documentation) increases delays.
  - Lack of a digital front-end causes redundant data entry and communication inefficiencies.
  - Batch or serial processing of cases instead of case-based, real-time handling.

- **B. High Process Variability and Rework:**
  - No clear triage mechanism to route cases by complexity at intake, causing unnecessary thorough examinations or repeated casual checks.
  - Reinitiation steps indicate unclear or incomplete initial investigations, leading to loops and redundant activities.

- **C. Inefficient Resource Utilization:**
  - Role-based restrictions are not digitally enforced, risking misallocation and handover delays.
  - Highly qualified resources (Sara, Sean) are involved late or inefficiently, with no digital pre-filtering or prioritization.
  - No case manager to coordinate and streamline case progression.

# 3. Redesigned Process Flow

## a. Digital Front-End and Workflow Automation (Apply Integral Technology)
- **Implement a digital customer portal** for warranty requests, capturing all required data and documentation at the outset.
- **Automate ticket checking** using workflow management software, flagging only exceptions for manual review.
- **Integrate a digital workflow engine** to enforce compliance restrictions (e.g., only Sara can decide, only Sean can investigate thoroughly).

## b. Activity Triage and Case-Based Routing (Apply Triage)
- **Introduce automated triage at intake:** 
  - Use rule-based logic (supported by the digital portal) to classify cases as "simple" or "complex" based on provided data.
  - Route "simple" cases for casual examination and fast-track decisioning.
  - Route "complex" or incomplete cases directly for thorough examination by Sean, skipping redundant casual checks.

## c. Case Manager Assignment (Appoint Case Managers)
- **Assign a dedicated case manager** (e.g., Pete, Mike, Ellen, or Sue) for each request at registration.
- The case manager is responsible for:
  - Monitoring case progress,
  - Ensuring timely handoffs to Sara and Sean,
  - Communicating proactively with the customer to reduce complaints about delays.

## d. Eliminate Redundant Activities and Rework (Eliminate Activities)
- **Remove unnecessary reinitiation loops** by:
  - Ensuring all required information is collected upfront via the portal.
  - Enforcing a single, clear investigation path per case (either casual or thorough, not both in sequence).
- **Digitally enforce the rule** that the ticket must be checked before any examination, preventing process violations and redundant steps.

## e. Parallelization Where Allowed (Resequence Activities)
- **Parallelize documentation and notification tasks** (e.g., preparing compensation or rejection letters) with the decision step, triggered as soon as Sara makes a decision.

## f. Real-Time Performance Monitoring (Apply Integral Technology)
- **Implement real-time dashboards** for case managers and process owners to identify and address emerging bottlenecks immediately.

## g. Green Process Variant (Offer Green Process Variant)
- **Offer a digital-only, paperless process option** to customers, reducing environmental impact and further streamlining the workflow.

### **Optimized Process Flow (Concrete Steps):**
1. **Customer submits request via digital portal** (mandatory data fields, document upload).
2. **Automated triage classifies case** as simple (casual exam) or complex (thorough exam).
3. **Case manager assigned** at registration, monitors and coordinates process.
4. **Automated ticket check** (exceptions flagged for manual review).
5. - **If simple:** Examine casually → Decision by Sara.
   - **If complex:** Examine thoroughly (Sean) → Decision by Sara.
6. **Decision triggers parallel tasks:** 
   - If compensation: Prepare payment and notify customer.
   - If rejection: Prepare rejection notification.
7. **Case manager ensures timely completion and customer communication.**
8. **Case closed.**

# 4. Expected Performance Improvements

- **Throughput Time Reduction:** 
  - Digital intake and triage eliminate manual data entry and reduce handover delays. Expected reduction: 30–50% in average case processing time.
  - Automated ticket checks and pre-filtering reduce time spent on routine cases.
- **Rework Reduction:** 
  - Upfront triage and complete data collection reduce reinitiation loops and redundant examinations by at least 80%.
- **Resource Utilization:**
  - Specialized resources (Sara, Sean) focus only on cases requiring their expertise, increasing their throughput and reducing bottlenecks.
  - Case managers ensure cases do not stall, improving flow and customer satisfaction.
- **Process Variant Reduction:**
  - Clear, digitally enforced routing reduces process variants from 6 to 2–3 well-defined paths.
- **Customer Satisfaction:**
  - Digital portal and proactive communication reduce complaints about delays.
- **Environmental Impact:**
  - Digital-only process variant reduces paper usage and aligns with Siemens’ sustainability strategy.
