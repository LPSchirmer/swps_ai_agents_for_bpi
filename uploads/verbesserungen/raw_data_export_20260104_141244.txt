**Cost Drivers Found:** 3
---
## 1. Identified Cost Drivers

- **A. High Frequency of Rework and Loops:**  
  Multiple process variants involve reinitiation of requests and repeated examination/check ticket activities, increasing labor and resource consumption.

- **B. Excessive Process Variant Complexity:**  
  Six process variants for only six cases, indicating lack of standardization and unnecessary process branching, leading to overhead in coordination and resource allocation.

- **C. Manual and Redundant Ticket Checking:**  
  The "check ticket" activity occurs 9 times in 6 cases, suggesting repeated manual work that could be streamlined or automated.

---

## 2. Root Cause Analysis

- **A. High Frequency of Rework and Loops:**  
  Root Cause: Lack of effective triage and insufficient information gathering at the initial registration step results in cases being reinitiated and re-examined. This causes redundant work and drives up labor costs.

- **B. Excessive Process Variant Complexity:**  
  Root Cause: Absence of clear case differentiation and standardized subflows causes the process to branch unnecessarily, increasing the number of unique paths and associated management overhead.

- **C. Manual and Redundant Ticket Checking:**  
  Root Cause: Ticket checking is performed repeatedly, likely due to missing automation and lack of integrated information systems. This leads to duplicated manual efforts.

---

## 3. Redesigned Process Flow

### **Redesign Patterns Applied:**
- Apply Triage
- Eliminate Activities
- Apply Integral Technology
- Establish a Case-Based Mindset

### **Concrete Redesigned Process:**

1. **Early Comprehensive Triage at Registration**
   - At "register request," implement a structured triage form (digital, mandatory fields) to capture all relevant information upfront.
   - Assign cases as "simple" or "complex" based on predefined criteria (e.g., claim value, documentation completeness, customer segment).

2. **Direct Routing Based on Triage Outcome**
   - **Simple Cases:**  
     - Route directly to "examine casually" → "decide" (Sara) → "pay compensation" or "reject request."  
     - Skip "check ticket" unless triage flags missing/invalid documentation.
   - **Complex Cases:**  
     - Route to "check ticket" (automated pre-check via document management system).  
     - If passed, proceed to "examine thoroughly" (Sean) → "decide" (Sara) → outcome.
     - If failed, auto-reject or request missing info before proceeding.

3. **Automate Ticket Checking**
   - Implement a document management system to auto-validate ticket completeness and flag issues.
   - Only escalate to manual check if automation fails or exceptions are detected.

4. **Eliminate Unnecessary Loops and Rework**
   - Standardize process flows:  
     - Disallow reinitiation unless customer provides new, materially different information.
     - All information requests are handled in a single, consolidated customer interaction.

5. **Reduce Process Variants**
   - Standardize to two main flows:  
     - *Simple* (casual examination, direct decision)  
     - *Complex* (automated check, thorough examination, decision)
   - Remove all other variants unless compliance requires.

6. **Case-Based Processing**
   - Each case handled individually, no batching or periodic review.
   - Assign a case manager for coordination (if volume justifies), but keep decision authority with Sara and thorough examination with Sean as per compliance.

- **A. High Process Throughput Time**
  - *Root Cause:* Sequential task execution, handover delays due to strict role compliance (e.g., only Sara can decide, only Sean can examine thoroughly), and lack of early information completeness.
- **B. Excessive Process Variants and Loops**
  - *Root Cause:* Insufficient triage at the beginning leads to cases being misrouted or incompletely processed, resulting in unnecessary reinitiations and repeated examinations.
- **C. Rework and Redundant Checks**
  - *Root Cause:* Information required for thorough examination is often incomplete at the start, causing repeated "check ticket" and "examine" activities. Lack of digital support for information gathering and validation further exacerbates this.

## Concrete, Implementable Changes

### a) Early and Comprehensive Information Capture (Apply Integral Technology, Eliminate Activities)
- **Change:** At "register request," implement a digital intake form that requires all necessary information and documentation upfront. The system validates completeness before allowing the case to proceed.
- **Implementation:** Use Siemens’ digitalization capabilities to automate data capture and validation. Only complete cases move forward, reducing later rework.

### b) Triage and Direct Routing (Apply Triage, Establish a Case-Based Mindset)
- **Change:** Introduce an automated triage step immediately after registration:
  - If the request is straightforward (criteria defined based on historical data), route directly to "examine casually."
  - If complexity or missing information is detected, route to "examine thoroughly" (Sean) directly, skipping casual examination and minimizing loops.
- **Implementation:** Use workflow rules to classify cases at intake and assign the correct path. All ticket checks are performed digitally before any examination.

### c) Parallelize Non-Decision Tasks Where Possible (Apply Integral Technology)
- **Change:** While compliance dictates only Sara can decide and only Sean can examine thoroughly, digital ticket checking and documentation can occur in parallel with the initial triage.
- **Implementation:** As soon as a request is registered, the system pre-fetches and checks ticket data, so this information is ready for the examiner without delay.

### d) Eliminate Redundant Reinitiations and Rechecks (Eliminate Activities, Apply Triage)
- **Change:** Redesign the process so that "reinitiate request" is only possible if new, substantive information is provided. The system blocks unnecessary re-entry into the examination loop.
- **Implementation:** Workflow management system enforces that reinitiation can only occur with new evidence or customer input, preventing circular flows.

### e) Digital Workflow Management and Task Assignment (Apply Integral Technology, Appoint Case Managers)
- **Change:** Implement a workflow management system that:
  - Assigns cases to resources based on role restrictions (Sara for decisions, Sean for thorough exams).
  - Tracks case progress and flags bottlenecks or delays in real time.
  - Optionally, appoints a "case manager" (not decision-maker) to oversee progress and ensure timely escalation.
- **Implementation:** Leverage Siemens’ existing digital platforms to roll out workflow automation and dashboarding.

### f) Industry Best Practice: Digital Complaint Handling (researched)
- **Change:** Benchmarking against ISO 10002 and leading industrial complaint management, integrate customer self-service portals for status tracking and additional information upload, reducing inbound status inquiries and administrative delays.
- **Implementation:** Customers can check status and provide missing info online, triggering immediate workflow updates.
