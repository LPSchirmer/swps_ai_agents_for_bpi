```
## 1. Relevant Legal & Regulatory Norms

- **ISO 10002:2018** (Quality management — Customer satisfaction — Guidelines for complaints handling in organizations)
- **Segregation of Duties (SoD) Principles** (internal control, commonly required by SOX, German HGB, and global best practices)
- **GDPR** (General Data Protection Regulation, for customer data handling in the EU)
- **Internal Siemens Compliance Policies** (as implied by explicit process restrictions)
- **Industry best practices** for warranty handling and customer complaint management

## 2. Compliance Assessment

The process "Garantieabwicklung" (warranty handling) at Siemens is evaluated against the above norms and explicit internal restrictions:

- **ISO 10002** requires transparent, prompt, and customer-oriented complaint handling, with clear responsibilities, documentation, and escalation mechanisms.
- **Segregation of Duties**: The process mandates that the person who handles a case cannot decide its outcome, and only specific individuals are authorized for critical steps (Sara for decisions, Sean for thorough investigations).
- **Process Sequence Restriction**: The ticket must always be checked before any investigation.
- **Data Handling**: No explicit privacy/data issues are indicated, but GDPR applies by default.
- **Customer Orientation**: Customer complaints about slow throughput and high cost indicate a gap in meeting ISO 10002’s efficiency and customer satisfaction goals.

## 3. Identified Violations & Gaps

1. **Segregation of Duties Violation**
   - The process data does not specify which resource performed each activity, but with only 6 resources and multiple activities per case, there is a risk that the same person may both handle and decide a case, violating explicit internal controls and SoD requirements.
   - There is no evidence that only Sara is making decisions or only Sean is conducting thorough investigations.

2. **Role Authorization Breach**
   - The process allows for "decide" and "examine thoroughly" to be performed in multiple variants, but without explicit enforcement that only Sara and Sean, respectively, perform these activities, there is a compliance gap.

3. **Process Sequence Violation**
   - The explicit rule states: "Das jeweilige Ticket muss immer erst begutachtet werden, bevor die Untersuchung eingeleitet wird." (The ticket must always be checked before the investigation starts.)
   - In the variant: `"register request, examine thoroughly, check ticket, decide, reject request"`, "examine thoroughly" occurs before "check ticket", which violates this rule.

4. **ISO 10002 Non-Conformance: Timeliness & Cost**
   - Customer complaints about slow and expensive processing indicate a failure to meet ISO 10002 requirements for promptness and efficiency in complaint handling.

## 4. Compliance Improvement Recommendations

### 4.1 Enforce Segregation of Duties & Role Restrictions

- **Implement strict role-based access controls** in the workflow system:
  - Only Sara can execute the "decide" activity.
  - Only Sean can execute the "examine thoroughly" activity.
  - Systematically log and audit user actions to ensure compliance.

- **Introduce automated workflow checks**:
  - The system must block any attempt by the same user to both handle and decide a case.
  - If a user has performed any handling activity, the "decide" activity must be routed to Sara only.

### 4.2 Correct Process Sequencing

- **Redesign process variants** to enforce the correct order:
  - The workflow engine must require "check ticket" to be completed before either "examine casually" or "examine thoroughly" can begin.
  - Remove or prohibit any process paths where investigation precedes ticket checking.

### 4.3 ISO 10002 Compliance: Timeliness & Cost

- **Apply process mining and automation**:
  - Use digital case management to minimize manual handoffs and reduce throughput time.
  - Assign tasks based on workload and quality metrics to optimize resource utilization and reduce bottlenecks.
  - Implement a "first contact resolution" principle where possible, collecting all necessary information upfront.

- **Monitor and report KPIs**:
  - Track average case duration, number of reinitiations, and resource utilization.
  - Set targets for resolution time and cost per case, aligned with ISO 10002.

### 4.4 Additional Recommendations

- **Customer Transparency**: Provide customers with real-time status updates and digital self-service options to reduce complaints about process duration.
- **Continuous Compliance Monitoring**: Regularly audit process logs for SoD, role, and sequence violations.
- **Documentation & Training**: Update SOPs and train staff on new compliance requirements and process changes.
```

**Cost Drivers Found:** 3
---
## 1. Identified Cost Drivers

1. **High Frequency of Manual Ticket Checks and Examinations**
    - The activity `check ticket` occurs 9 times for only 6 cases, indicating repeated or redundant checks.
    - Both `examine casually` and `examine thoroughly` are performed multiple times, sometimes within the same case, leading to duplicated effort.

2. **Reinitiation and Rework Loops**
    - The process variant data shows that some cases cycle through `reinitiate request`, followed by further examinations and ticket checks, significantly increasing resource use per case.

3. **Resource Fragmentation and Lack of Digital Integration**
    - Multiple resources (6 named individuals) are involved in a small number of cases, with strict role assignments (e.g., only Sara can decide, only Sean can examine thoroughly).
    - Absence of digital workflow or self-service options increases coordination effort and manual handoffs.

---

## 2. Root Cause Analysis

1. **Manual and Redundant Activities**
    - The process requires every ticket to be checked before any investigation, but the lack of digital integration means this step is repeated across reinitiations and between casual and thorough examinations.
    - No clear triage or case differentiation at intake, leading to unnecessary thoroughness or repetition for straightforward cases.

2. **Rework and Process Loops**
    - Cases are often reinitiated after an initial decision, causing additional rounds of examination and ticket checking.
    - This is likely due to incomplete information gathering at the start, or lack of clear criteria for escalation between casual and thorough examinations.

3. **Inefficient Resource Utilization**
    - The process structure requires multiple handoffs and involvement of specialized resources even for simple cases.
    - No case manager or digital workflow to streamline task assignment or reduce unnecessary touchpoints.

---

## 3. Redesigned Process Flow

### Patterns Applied:
- **Apply Integral Technology**
- **Apply Triage**
- **Appoint Case Managers**
- **Eliminate Activities**
- **Establish a Case-Based Mindset**
- **One-and-Done Principle**

### Concrete Redesign Steps:

1. **Digital Intake and Pre-Triage**
    - Implement a digital self-service portal for warranty requests.
    - Collect all necessary information and documentation at intake, including ticket details, supporting evidence, and customer information.
    - Use automated rules to triage cases into “simple” (eligible for casual examination) and “complex” (require thorough examination) categories.

2. **Automated Ticket Verification**
    - Integrate a workflow automation tool to perform initial ticket checks automatically.
    - Only escalate to manual ticket checking if the automated check flags an exception.

3. **Direct Assignment and Case Management**
    - Assign each case to a dedicated case manager (not the decision-maker or thorough examiner) responsible for end-to-end coordination.
    - The case manager ensures all required documentation is complete before routing to the appropriate resource (Sara for decision, Sean for thorough examination).

4. **Eliminate Redundant Rework Loops**
    - Enforce a “one-and-done” principle: All required information must be gathered and validated before any examination begins.
    - Reinitiation is only allowed if new, externally triggered information becomes available, not due to internal process gaps.

5. **Simplified Process Variants**
    - For simple cases (as determined by triage), proceed: `register request → automated ticket check → examine casually → decide (Sara) → pay compensation/reject`.
    - For complex cases: `register request → automated ticket check → examine thoroughly (Sean) → decide (Sara) → pay compensation/reject`.
    - Remove all unnecessary intermediate checks and reinitiations unless mandated by compliance.

6. **Digital Handoffs and Notifications**
    - All handoffs and status updates managed digitally, reducing manual communication and coordination overhead.

```

Triage: Simple or Complex?
    ↓

[Complex] → Assign case manager → Examine thoroughly (Sean) → Decide (Sara) → Pay compensation/Reject
```

**Performance Issues Found:** 3

# 1. Identified Performance Issues

- **1.1 High Process Variability and Rework:** Multiple process variants with frequent reinitiation and repeated activities (e.g., "reinitiate request", "examine casually", "check ticket") indicate rework and inconsistent case handling.
- **1.2 Slow Throughput and Customer Complaints:** Explicit feedback that the process is "zu langsam" (too slow), with customers complaining about long wait times.
- **1.3 Manual, Fragmented Task Assignment:** Activities are distributed across multiple resources, with strict role-based restrictions, but without clear case ownership or digital support, leading to delays and coordination overhead.

# 2. Root Cause Analysis

- **2.1 High Process Variability and Rework**
  - Lack of initial triage or differentiation leads to cases being handled inappropriately, resulting in rework (e.g., cases first examined casually, then escalated to thorough examination after decision/reinitiation).
  - No standardized interface for case registration, causing incomplete or unclear information and subsequent rework.
- **2.2 Slow Throughput and Customer Complaints**
  - Absence of digital workflow management and automation means manual handoffs, status tracking, and coordination, increasing wait times.
  - Batch processing or ad-hoc task allocation (without case-based mindset) causes cases to wait for resource availability rather than being processed immediately.
- **2.3 Manual, Fragmented Task Assignment**
  - No case manager or digital routing system; tasks are assigned per activity, not per case, leading to delays, coordination issues, and lack of accountability.
  - Strict compliance rules (e.g., only Sara can decide, only Sean can examine thoroughly) are handled manually, causing bottlenecks if these resources are unavailable.

# 3. Redesigned Process Flow

## 3.1 Apply Triage and Standardized Interface at Intake

- **Digital Self-Service Portal:** Implement a digital portal for customers to register warranty requests, enforcing mandatory fields and standardized data formats. This reduces errors and incomplete submissions.
- **Automated Triage Logic:** Upon submission, the portal uses rule-based triage to classify cases as "simple" or "complex" based on input data.
  - *Simple cases* are routed for "examine casually".
  - *Complex cases* (or those with missing/ambiguous info) are flagged for direct "examine thoroughly" by Sean, bypassing unnecessary casual examination.

## 3.2 Digital Workflow Management and Case-Based Assignment

- **Workflow Automation Platform:** Deploy a workflow management system that:
  - Digitally routes each case through required activities, ensuring compliance with role restrictions (e.g., only Sara can perform "decide", only Sean can "examine thoroughly").
  - Assigns a "Case Manager" (e.g., Ellen or Pete) for each case at registration, responsible for end-to-end progression and communication.
  - Provides real-time status tracking for all stakeholders, including customers.
- **Immediate Task Assignment:** Each activity is triggered as soon as the previous one is complete, eliminating batch processing and manual queues.

## 3.3 Eliminate Redundant Rework and Optimize Sequence

- **Direct Escalation:** If a case is not resolved after the first decision (e.g., if reinitiation is required), the workflow system automatically escalates it to "examine thoroughly" (if not already done), skipping repeated casual examination/checks.
- **Automated Notifications and Handoffs:** System notifies the next responsible resource instantly upon task completion, reducing handoff delays.

## 3.4 Industry Best Practices Integration

- **Self-Service Tracking:** Customers can track case status online, reducing inquiry calls and perceived delays.
- **Digital Document Management:** All tickets and supporting documents are uploaded and accessible to all authorized resources, minimizing information search time.

## 3.5 Concrete Redesigned Flow

```mermaid
    F --> G

    G -- Approve --> I[Pay compensation, notify customer, close case]
    G -- Need more info --> J[Reinitiate request, Case Manager gathers info, return to D]
```

- **Reduction in Rework:** Automated triage and direct escalation reduce unnecessary repeated activities, decreasing average case handling steps by at least 1–2 per complex case.
- **Faster Throughput:** Digital workflow and immediate routing eliminate manual handoffs and batch queues, expected to reduce end-to-end cycle time by 30–50%.
- **Improved Customer Satisfaction:** Self-service portal and tracking provide transparency, reducing complaints about process duration.
- **Enhanced Accountability:** Case Manager role ensures each case is proactively managed, minimizing idle time and coordination delays.
- **Industry Alignment:** The redesign leverages Siemens’ digitalization strategy and aligns with best practices in warranty management for industrial companies.
