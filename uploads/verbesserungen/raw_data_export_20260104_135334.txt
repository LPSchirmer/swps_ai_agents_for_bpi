## 1. Relevant Legal & Regulatory Norms

- **ISO 10002:2018** – Quality management – Customer satisfaction – Guidelines for complaints handling in organizations (explicitly required)
- **Segregation of Duties (SoD) Principles** – Internal control standards for fraud prevention and process integrity
- **EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)** – Data privacy and handling (as Siemens is EU-based)
- **German Commercial Code (HGB)** – Documentation and audit trail requirements for business processes
- **Siemens Internal Compliance Policies** – Especially regarding roles, responsibilities, and process documentation

## 2. Compliance Assessment

The provided process description, KPIs, and explicit restrictions were assessed against the above norms:

- **ISO 10002**: The process must ensure transparency, timely handling, documented steps, and impartial decision-making.
- **SoD Principles**: Explicit process restrictions require separation between those who process and those who decide.
- **GDPR**: No explicit personal data handling described, but process must ensure only authorized personnel access customer data.
- **HGB**: Requires all process steps, decisions, and handovers to be traceable and documented.
- **Internal Policies**: Restrictions mandate role-based task assignment and sequencing of activities.

## 3. Identified Violations & Gaps

**1. Segregation of Duties Violation**
   - *Restriction*: "Die Person, welche das Anliegen bearbeitet hat, darf nicht entscheiden, was damit passiert."
   - *Issue*: Process variants do not specify or enforce resource separation for "examine" and "decide" steps. Without explicit workflow control, same person could perform both.

**2. Role-Based Assignment Violation**
   - *Restriction*: "Nur Sara darf entscheiden." / "Die gründliche Untersuchung darf nur von Sean durchgeführt werden."
   - *Issue*: Variants and resource list do not show enforcement. Without system-enforced assignment, other users could perform restricted activities.

**3. Activity Sequencing Violation**
   - *Restriction*: "Das jeweilige Ticket muss immer erst begutachtet werden, bevor die Untersuchung eingeleitet wird."
   - *Issue*: Some variants (e.g., "register request, examine thoroughly, check ticket...") show "examine thoroughly" before "check ticket," violating the mandated order.

**4. Documentation & Audit Trail Gaps**
   - *ISO 10002 & HGB*: No evidence of systematic documentation of who performed each activity, decision rationale, or customer communication logs.

## 4. Compliance Improvement Recommendations

**A. Enforce Segregation of Duties**
   - Implement workflow automation that prevents the same resource from performing both the "examine" (casually or thoroughly) and "decide" steps.
   - Systematically log resource assignments for auditability.

**B. Strict Role-Based Task Assignment**
   - Configure process management software to:
     - Assign all "decide" activities exclusively to Sara.
     - Assign all "examine thoroughly" activities exclusively to Sean.
   - Restrict access to these activities for all other resources.

**C. Correct Activity Sequencing**
   - Redesign process flows so that "check ticket" always precedes any examination activity.
   - Update process variants to eliminate any sequence where examination occurs before ticket checking.
   - Implement validation rules in workflow tools to enforce this sequence.

**D. Enhance Documentation and Audit Trail**
   - Ensure every process case logs:
     - Who performed each activity
     - Timestamp of each step
     - Rationale for decisions (especially for compensation/rejection)
     - All customer communications (to meet ISO 10002 transparency)
   - Regularly review logs for compliance and improvement opportunities.

**E. Support ISO 10002 Compliance**
   - Reduce process throughput time by:
     - Early triage of requests (casual vs. thorough examination) to optimize resource allocation
     - Minimize customer touchpoints by collecting all required information upfront and providing proactive status updates
   - Monitor KPIs such as case duration, customer satisfaction, and first-contact resolution rate.

**F. GDPR/Data Privacy Considerations**
   - Ensure only authorized personnel access customer data in line with their roles.
   - Document consent and data handling steps where applicable.

## 1. Identified Cost Drivers

- **A. Rework and Loops:** Multiple process variants include reinitiation of requests and repeated examination/checking steps (e.g., "reinitiate request", "examine casually", "check ticket" repeated), leading to duplicated work and increased labor cost.
- **B. Overuse of Specialized Resources:** "Examine thoroughly" (performed only by Sean) and "decide" (only by Sara) are bottlenecked, with specialized, likely higher-cost resources involved in cases that may not require such expertise.
- **C. Fragmented Task Assignment:** Task allocation does not appear optimized for cost, with no evidence of triage or early separation of simple vs. complex cases, causing unnecessary workload on higher-cost staff and redundant activities.

## 2. Root Cause Analysis

- **A. Rework and Loops:** Lack of effective triage at process entry means cases are not routed based on complexity. Simple cases may escalate unnecessarily, and unclear criteria for "examine casually" vs. "examine thoroughly" lead to process loops and rework.
- **B. Overuse of Specialized Resources:** Absence of early filtering means specialized staff (Sean, Sara) are engaged in cases that could be resolved at a lower level, increasing labor costs per case.
- **C. Fragmented Task Assignment:** No digital workflow or automation to enforce cost-efficient routing. Manual assignment increases handoffs, idle time, and risk of suboptimal resource utilization.

## 3. Redesigned Process Flow

### **Concrete, Cost-Reducing Process Design for "Garantieabwicklung" at Siemens**

**Step 1: Digital Request Registration & Automated Triage**
- All warranty requests are registered via a digital portal.
- An AI-driven or rule-based triage system classifies cases as "simple" (likely eligible for casual examination and quick decision) or "complex" (potential need for thorough examination).

**Step 2: Early Ticket Check (Automated Where Possible)**
- For "simple" cases, ticket check is performed by the system or lowest-cost qualified resource (e.g., Pete, Mike, Ellen, Sue).
- For "complex" cases, ticket check is still required but flagged for potential escalation.

**Step 3: Streamlined Case Handling**
- **Simple Cases:** After ticket check, proceed directly to "examine casually" (handled by available generalist staff), then to "decide" (Sara), minimizing handoffs and eliminating loops.
- **Complex Cases:** After ticket check, route to "examine thoroughly" (Sean), then to "decide" (Sara).
- **No Reinitiation Loops:** If additional information is needed, system prompts for all required data upfront at registration, minimizing back-and-forth.

**Step 4: Digital Decision and Communication**
- Sara reviews only the filtered, triaged cases requiring decision.
- Decision outcomes (pay compensation or reject request) are communicated automatically to the customer, reducing manual effort.

**Step 5: Process Monitoring and Exception Handling**
- Only genuine exceptions (unclear cases, missing information) are flagged for manual intervention; otherwise, the digital workflow handles routing and notifications.

**Process Redesign Patterns Applied:**
- *Triage/Early Differentiation*: Separate simple from complex cases at entry.
- *Role-based Task Assignment*: Assign tasks strictly per compliance, but only after triage.
- *Reduce Touchpoints*: Collect all required information at registration, combine customer contacts.
- *Automation/Digital Workflow*: Use digital tools for ticket check, routing, and communication.
- *Eliminate Rework*: No loops; ensure all info is collected upfront and cases are resolved in a single pass.

## **Stepwise Optimized Process Design**

### 1. **Digital Front-End with Standardized Interface**
- Implement a digital portal for "register request" with mandatory structured fields and document upload.
- Use validation rules to ensure completeness and clarity, reducing the need for reinitiation.

### 2. **Automated Case Triage at Registration**
- After digital registration, an automated triage engine classifies cases as "simple" (eligible for casual examination) or "complex" (require thorough examination).
- Triage is based on predefined business rules (e.g., claim type, value, documentation completeness).

### 3. **Parallel Preparation and Ticket Checking**
- Immediately after triage, "check ticket" is triggered in parallel with preparation for examination (casual or thorough), provided compliance is maintained (ticket must be checked before examination starts).
- Use workflow automation to ensure ticket checking is completed and examination cannot proceed until this prerequisite is met.

### 4. **Role-Based Task Assignment with Load Balancing**
- For "examine casually," assign cases dynamically to available qualified staff (excluding Sara and Sean, if not allowed).
- For "examine thoroughly," route only to Sean, but pre-schedule and bundle these tasks to minimize idle time.
- "Decide" is always routed to Sara, but the workflow system prioritizes cases with completed examinations to avoid Sara’s idle time.

### 5. **Automated Status Updates and Customer Communication**
- At each major step (registration, triage, examination, decision), automated notifications are sent to the customer, reducing inbound status requests and improving transparency.

### 6. **Elimination of Unnecessary Rework**
- If a request is incomplete, the digital portal prompts the customer for missing information before submission, eliminating the "reinitiate request" loop for preventable errors.
- If reinitiation is required for legitimate reasons, the workflow system ensures only the missing step is repeated, not the entire process.

### 7. **Digital Workflow Monitoring**
- Real-time dashboards for managers to identify pending tasks, bottlenecks, and resource utilization, enabling proactive intervention.
