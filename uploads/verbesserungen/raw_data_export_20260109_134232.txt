## 1. Relevant Legal & Regulatory Norms

- **ISO 10002:2018/2024 (Quality management — Customer satisfaction — Guidelines for complaints handling in organizations)**
  - *Key requirements:*
    - Clearly defined and communicated complaints-handling process
    - Accessibility and transparency for complainants
    - Timely acknowledgment, investigation, and resolution of complaints
    - Objectivity and independence in complaint investigation and decision-making
    - Roles, responsibilities, and authorities clearly assigned and separated
    - Continuous improvement and learning from complaints
    - Data protection and confidentiality

- **EU Consumer Rights Directive (2011/83/EU, as amended) and German Civil Code (BGB) on Warranty Handling**
  - *Key requirements:*
    - Warranty claims must be processed within reasonable timeframes (generally 14-30 days for response/decision)
    - Customers must not be required to provide information repeatedly
    - Transparent, fair, and accessible complaint and warranty procedures
    - Clear assignment of responsibilities in complaint resolution
    - Data privacy and protection in all customer interactions

- **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)**
  - *Key requirements:*
    - Lawful, fair, and transparent processing of personal data
    - Data minimization and purpose limitation
    - Appropriate technical and organizational measures for data security

  - *Timeliness*: The process is described as slow with customer complaints about duration, violating the timeliness principle.
  - *Transparency/Accessibility*: No evidence of a single-point-of-contact or minimized customer touchpoints, risking non-compliance with accessibility and transparency requirements.
  - *Role clarity*: Role assignments are not strictly enforced in process execution (e.g., "decide" activity performed by Sue and Sean, not only Sara).

- **EU/German Warranty Law Violations:**
  - *Timely Processing*: Customer complaints about long duration indicate non-compliance with the requirement to process warranty claims within a reasonable time.
  - *Single Interaction Principle*: The process allows for multiple "reinitiate request" and "examine" loops, risking repeated customer contacts and information requests, contrary to EU requirements.

- **GDPR Violations:**
  - No explicit evidence of data protection controls in the process, risking non-compliance with GDPR requirements for data minimization and security.

## 1. Redesigned Process Flow
- Upon receiving a warranty request, the process starts with `register request`. All customer information and required documentation are collected in a single interaction, using a digital self-service portal to minimize manual input and reduce repeated data entry.
- The system automatically performs a pre-check using rule-based triage to determine whether the case is simple (standard claim, clear documentation) or complex (ambiguous, incomplete, or high-value claim).
    - *Simple cases*: Directly routed for "check ticket" and, if criteria are met, proceed to "examine casually".
    - *Complex cases*: Routed for "check ticket" and then to "examine thoroughly" (Sean), if needed.
- "Check ticket" is performed by the system for simple cases (automated validation of data completeness and eligibility). For complex cases, Pete or Mike perform the check.
- "Examine casually" is performed only for simple cases, by Mike or Ellen, as assigned by workload.
- "Examine thoroughly" is reserved for complex cases and performed exclusively by Sean.
- After examination, all cases are routed to Sara for "decide". The system ensures the decision-maker is not the same as the handler.
- If additional information is needed, the system triggers a single "reinitiate request" loop, but only once per case, with automated reminders and digital data capture to prevent repeated rework.
- After decision:
    - If approved, "pay compensation" is executed by Ellen (for simple cases) or Mike (for complex cases).
    - If rejected, "reject request" is executed by Pete.
- All process steps are logged and monitored via an integrated workflow management system for transparency and to support further automation.

## 2. Assigned resources
1. **Sara**: Sole decision-maker for all cases (`decide`).
2. **Sean**: Handles all "examine thoroughly" activities for complex cases only.
3. **Mike**: Performs "check ticket" (complex), "examine casually" (simple), and "pay compensation" (complex).
4. **Ellen**: Performs "examine casually" (simple) and "pay compensation" (simple).
5. **Pete**: Handles "check ticket" (complex) and "reject request".
6. **Digital Workflow System**: Automates "register request" intake, initial "check ticket" for simple cases, triage, reminders, and data validation.

## 3. Major changes
- Introduction of digital self-service portal and workflow automation to reduce manual data entry and repeated customer contacts.
- Application of rule-based triage to separate simple and complex cases, minimizing unnecessary thorough examinations and manual checks.
- Automated, system-based pre-check for simple cases, reducing resource usage for repetitive validation.
- Strict limitation of "reinitiate request" (rework) to a single loop per case, with digital reminders and data capture to prevent cost-intensive repeated rework.
- Streamlined resource assignment based on case complexity and workload, eliminating redundant manual activities.

## 4. Used process redesign patterns
1. **Apply triage**
2. **Apply integral technology**
3. **Workload-based task assignment**
4. **One-stop (first-time-right)**
5. **Appoint case managers** (Sara as decision authority)
6. **Work in customer teams** (resource collaboration for complex cases)

## 1. Redesigned Process Flow

- All warranty requests are registered via a standardized digital portal, enforcing required data fields and automated validation to reduce incomplete submissions.
- Upon registration, the system automatically checks ticket validity (as required) and routes the case based on a triage logic:
    - *Simple/standard requests* (predefined by rules, e.g., clear documentation, low value, non-complex) are routed for "casual examination".
    - *Complex/ambiguous requests* (flagged by missing data, high value, prior issues, or system-detected anomalies) are routed for "thorough examination".
- The triage logic is embedded into the workflow management system and dynamically assigns the appropriate examiner (Mike/Ellen/Sue for casual, Sean for thorough) based on experience and workload.
- After examination, the workflow system triggers the "decide" activity, which is always assigned to Sara (as required), with all case data and examination results precompiled and available in the system dashboard.
- If a request is rejected or compensation is paid, the system automatically closes the case and notifies the customer via the portal, providing standardized feedback and documentation.
- If rework is necessary (e.g., due to missing information or unclear findings), the system enforces a single reinitiation loop: the case is returned to the examiner with explicit instructions and a checklist of missing/required items, preventing multiple rework cycles.
- All activities are tracked in real-time; the workflow management system provides dashboards for bottleneck identification and automatic escalation if activities exceed predefined SLA thresholds.
- Case managers (Sue or Ellen) are assigned per case to monitor progress, coordinate between resources, and ensure timely resolution, but do not perform restricted activities themselves.

## 2. Assigned resources

1. **Sara**: Solely responsible for all "decide" activities.
2. **Sean**: Handles all "examine thoroughly" activities.
3. **Mike, Ellen, Sue**: Assigned to "examine casually" and "check ticket" activities, with dynamic allocation based on workload and experience.
4. **Pete**: Supports "register request" and "check ticket" activities.
5. **Ellen or Sue**: Assigned as case manager per case, responsible for monitoring, coordination, and escalation.
6. **Workflow management system**: Automates routing, triage, SLA monitoring, and customer notifications.

## 3. Major changes

- Digital portal with standardized, validated input replaces manual or inconsistent request intake.
- Automated triage logic directs cases to the appropriate examination path, reducing unnecessary thorough examinations and optimizing resource use.
- Workflow management system enforces single rework loop with explicit feedback, eliminating repeated rework cycles and reducing case duration variance.
- Dynamic, experience-based assignment of tasks ensures optimal resource utilization and reduces bottlenecks.
- Case manager role introduced for end-to-end oversight, without violating compliance restrictions.
- Real-time process tracking and automated escalation replace manual monitoring.

## 4. Used process redesign patterns

1. **Establish standardized interfaces**

4. **Experience-based task assignment**
5. **Appoint case managers**
